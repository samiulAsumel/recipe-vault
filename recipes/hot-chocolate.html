Spanish Hot Chocolate (Chocolate a la Taza)

Thick, pudding-like drinking chocolate made for dipping churros.

Vegetarian Gluten-Free Nut-Free
Prep5 min
Cook10 min
Total15 min
Servings 2 servings
DifficultyMedium

Milk and dark chocolate thickened with cornstarch into a spoon-coating, pudding-like drink -- traditionally served alongside churros for dipping, and deliberately less sweet than a mug of American hot chocolate.

Ingredients

  • 500 ml whole milk, divided
  • 100 g dark chocolate, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp sugar, or to taste -- this is meant to be less sweet than American hot chocolate
  • pinch of salt

To serve

  • churros, for dipping

Instructions

  3. Melting the chocolate over low heat, whisking constantly, keeps it from seizing or scorching against the bottom of the pan.

  5. 5 min

    It should be thick enough to coat the back of a spoon and hold a dipped churro -- if it still pours like a thin drink, it hasn't cooked long enough for the cornstarch to set up.

Notes

  • The cornstarch, not extra chocolate, is what gives this its signature pudding-like thickness -- that's what separates chocolate a la taza from a regular mug of hot chocolate.
  • Use the best dark chocolate you have -- this recipe has so few ingredients that the chocolate's quality is the whole drink.
  • No whipped cream or marshmallows -- the traditional pairing is churros, and the drink itself is kept on the less-sweet side.
