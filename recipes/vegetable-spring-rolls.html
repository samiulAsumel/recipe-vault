Vietnamese Spring Rolls (Chả Giò Chay)

Crisp fried rolls packed with wood ear mushroom, glass noodles, and vegetables.

Vegetarian Vegan Dairy-Free
Prep40 min
Cook15 min
Total55 min
Servings 6 servings
DifficultyMedium

The vegetarian version of Vietnam's fried spring roll -- wood ear mushroom, glass noodles, and shredded vegetables rolled tight and fried until crackling, meant to be wrapped in lettuce and herbs and dipped in a tangy nuoc cham.

Ingredients

For the filling

  • 12 spring roll wrappers
  • 10 g dried wood ear mushroom, soaked in hot water until soft, finely chopped
  • 30 g dried glass noodles, soaked until soft, cut into short lengths
  • 1 cup carrot, finely shredded
  • 1 cup jicama or taro, finely shredded
  • 3 shiitake mushrooms, finely diced
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • black pepper to taste
  • oil for frying

For the nuoc cham chay (vegetarian dipping sauce)

  • 3 tbsp soy sauce, in place of fish sauce
  • 2 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 3 tbsp warm water
  • 1 small red chili, minced
  • 1 clove garlic, minced

To serve

  • lettuce leaves and fresh herbs (mint, perilla), for wrapping

Instructions

  1. The filling should be finely chopped and well-drained of excess water -- a wet filling steams the wrapper from the inside and stops it from crisping.

  4. 5 min

    Frying at a steady 175C, not hotter, is what crisps the wrapper all the way through before the outside over-browns.

Notes

  • Traditional chả giò is often made with pork and shrimp -- this vegetarian version (chả giò chay) is a genuine, common variant, not an invented substitute, and is widely eaten at Buddhist vegetarian meals in Vietnam.
  • Rolls can be assembled ahead and refrigerated -- fry just before serving for maximum crunch.
  • Baking at 200C (400F) for 20 minutes, turning once, works as a lighter alternative to frying.
