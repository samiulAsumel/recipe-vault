Panta Bhat (Fermented Rice with Fried Ilish)

Overnight-soaked rice with fried hilsa, raw onion, and green chili -- Bengal's oldest breakfast.

Gluten-Free Dairy-Free Egg-Free Nut-Free
Prep15 min
Cook15 min
Total8 hr 30 min
Servings 2 servings
DifficultyEasy

Day-old rice soaked in water overnight until faintly sour and fermented, served cold or at room temperature with a drizzle of mustard oil, raw onion, green chilies, and a piece of fried Ilish (hilsa) -- the traditional farmer's breakfast eaten across Bengal for centuries, and now the centerpiece of Pohela Boishakh, the Bengali New Year.

Ingredients

For the panta bhat

  • 2 cups cooked rice, day-old
  • water, enough to submerge the rice
  • salt to taste

For the fried Ilish

  • 2 Ilish (hilsa) fish steaks, or a firm, fatty white fish as a substitute
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3 tbsp mustard oil, for frying

To serve

  • 1 small onion, finely sliced
  • 2 green chilies
  • 1 tbsp mustard oil, raw, to drizzle
  • lime wedge, optional

Instructions

  1. 480 min

    The overnight soak is what ferments the rice slightly, giving panta bhat its faintly sour tang -- this isn't the same as just adding cold water to rice right before serving.

  3. 10 min

    Ilish has a high fat content and delicate flesh, so resist moving it too soon -- let a real crust form before flipping or it will fall apart.

Notes

  • Panta bhat has been the everyday breakfast of Bengali farmers and laborers for centuries -- simple, cooling in the heat, and a way to use the previous night's rice rather than waste it.
  • On Pohela Boishakh, the Bengali New Year, panta bhat with fried Ilish and green chilies is served as a celebratory dish, even though it began as the plainest food of rural Bengal.
  • If Ilish isn't available, any firm, fatty fish fried the same way is a reasonable substitute -- Ilish itself is seasonal and can be hard to find outside Bengal.
